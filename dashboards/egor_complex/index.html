EGOR Complex Spectrum

The seven-vertex EGOR tree has seven real eigenvalues. Placed on the critical line as s = ½ + iλ, they can be tested against the Riemann zeta function. Each number on this page was recomputed to 40 digits in the CylicAmp repository.

EGOR is Michael Warren Song's work, computed in EGOR. The checks are in math/theorems/owner_egor_tree_complex_spectrum_audit_2026_11_16.py.

The tree and its block

The tree splits into two classes of 3 and 4 vertices, so its adjacency matrix is [[0, B], [Bᵀ, 0]]. The squared eigenvalues are the eigenvalues of B·Bᵀ.

Complex form and edge phases

  • H = [[0, iB], [−iBᵀ, 0]] is Hermitian. It equals the real matrix turned by diag(1, 1, 1, −i, −i, −i, −i), so it has the same seven eigenvalues.
  • Any complex phases on the six edges leave the polynomial unchanged. The repository checks 20 random draws. On a tree each phase can be moved onto a vertex and cancelled, because every pair of vertices has exactly one path between them.
  • This is a known fact about gain graphs: a connected gain graph has the plain graph's spectrum exactly when it is balanced, and a tree is always balanced (N. Reff, arXiv 1110.4554). Changing the spectrum with phases needs a cycle.

Where it goes next

  • Spectral route. A defined sequence of EGOR graphs or operators, their characteristic polynomials, and whether their spectra keep a stable relation to the zeta ordinates.
  • Dirichlet-series route. The finite-sieve Dirichlet series carried into the complex plane, with its zeros and poles, and bounds that hold as the sieve cutoff grows.
