Rule 30 Random

Random numbers from Rule 30, the cellular automaton Wolfram used for random integers in Mathematica. Pick a seed and the same seed always gives the same numbers, here and in the Python program.

The source

One black cell, 160 steps. Each cell becomes left XOR (center OR right). The marked column under the first cell runs … (OEIS A051023).

Make numbers

What it is

  • How. A ring of 1024 cells, filled from the seed by SHA-256, run 1024 steps to mix, then one bit per step read from the middle cell. Numbers in a range throw back draws that land past it, so every value is equally likely.
  • Checked. The center column matches OEIS A051023 for 100,000 cells. On six seeds the generator passes the NIST tests for monobit, block frequency, runs and serial, plus bytes, dice and autocorrelation. The statistics themselves match NIST's worked examples.
  • Good for games, simulations, sampling and Monte Carlo: anything that needs numbers that look random and can be repeated.
  • Not for passwords, keys or money. Meier and Staffelbach (1991) showed how to recover the cells from the output.
  • Not proved. Nobody has proved Rule 30 is random. All three Wolfram Rule 30 Prize problems are open. A ring of 1024 cells must repeat some day, and how long that takes is not known. Passing tests means no fault was seen.
