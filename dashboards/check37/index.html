Check 37

Adds one check character to any code of letters and digits, then catches every single wrong symbol and every swap of two symbols. It is the international standard ISO/IEC 7064 MOD 37-2, and it runs on arithmetic by 37.

The steps

Symbols are numbers: 0–9 are 0–9, A–Z are 10–35, and * is 36. Each step: total → 2 × total + symbol, keep what is left by 37. A code is right when the whole code, check included, ends on 1.

Why it catches mistakes

  • One wrong symbol moves the total by the difference times a power of 2. 37 is prime, so that is never a multiple of 37: always caught.
  • Two symbols swapped, d places apart, move it by (a − b) × a power of 2 × (2d − 1). 2 runs through all 36 remainders before it comes back to 1, so 2d − 1 is a multiple of 37 only when d is 36. Every swap in a code of up to 36 symbols is caught.
  • A doubled symbol mistyped (AA → BB) moves it by 3 × (a − b) × a power of 2: caught.
  • Several random mistakes at once slip through 1 time in 37, about 2.7%.
