Safe Primes

A Sophie Germain prime p has 2p + 1 prime too, and 2p + 1 is then a safe prime: 5 → 11, 11 → 23, 23 → 47. Key exchange between computers (Diffie–Hellman) runs on safe primes. This page makes new ones, checks any number, and lists them.

How it works

  • The table. Entry k says whether the odd number 2k + 1 is prime. For p = 2k + 1, the number 2p + 1 = 4k + 3 is entry 2k + 1. So a Sophie Germain prime is entries k and 2k + 1 both prime.
  • Always true. Above 3, p leaves 2 by 3 (if it left 1, 2p + 1 would be a multiple of 3), so p and 2p + 1 both have digit root 2, 5 or 8, and every safe prime above 7 leaves 11 by 12.
  • Making a big one. Start at a random p that leaves 5 by 6. Step up by 6 and cross out every p where p or 2p + 1 has a prime factor below 65,536. Test each survivor p with Miller–Rabin: 64 random rounds, so a composite passes with chance below 4−64. Below 3.3 × 1024 the bases 2 to 41 make it exact.
  • The proof for q. Once p is prime, q = 2p + 1 is proved prime (Pocklington): q is prime exactly when 2q−1 leaves 1 by q and 3 does not divide q.
  • For key exchange, use generator 4: it is a square, so it makes the group of size p. Generator 2 does the same when q leaves 7 by 8.

The same arithmetic as exports/safe_primes.py in the CylicAmp repository, whose tests match OEIS A092816 up to 107 and check five RFC 3526 and RFC 7919 key-exchange primes. Its 512-, 1024- and 2048-bit outputs were confirmed by sympy and OpenSSL.
