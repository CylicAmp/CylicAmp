Diamond Blocks

The digits 1 to 8 in four blocks: Evens, Odds, Primes and Non-primes. Each block is set as a diamond, with an outer pair and an inner pair. The Primes and Non-primes are each off balance by one in opposite directions, and together they balance at 18 and 18.

The blocks are the owner's. The diamonds and the cross-block reading came from another tool, and every line is checked in math/theorems/pasted_diamond_blocks_cross_balance_audit_2026_11_14.py.

Why it balances

Both cuts have the same outside and the same inside. The outside is 2, 8 + 1, 7 = 2, 7 + 1, 8, the four end digits 1 2 7 8. The inside is 4, 6 + 3, 5, the four middle digits 3 4 5 6. The digits pair to 9, as the arcs show: 1 + 8 = 2 + 7 = 3 + 6 = 4 + 5 = 9. That gives two pairs on each side, 18 each.

So the Prime / Non-prime cut is the Even / Odd cut with 7 and 8 traded between the blocks. The cores stay where they are: Odds and Primes share 3, 5 inside, and Evens and Non-primes share 4, 6.

Only one way to reach 18 | 18

Rows are the prime pair placed outside, and columns are the non-prime pair placed outside. Each cell is the outer total. 18 | 18 needs an outer total of 18 (1 + … + 8 = 36, half of it 18). Two cells reach it, and they are the same arrangement with outside and inside swapped.

The chain

1 + 2 + … + 8 = 36 → 36 / 2 = 18
2 + 3 + 5 + 7 = 17 → 17 + 18 = 35 → 3 + 5 = 8

Each step is right. 17 is the sum of the primes, and 18 is each side of the balance.

The digits of 35 match the shared core 3, 5, and the digits of 18 match the Non-prime outer pair 1, 8. Those are matches in how the numbers are written, so they're recorded as matches, not as a result.
