Prime Pairs Moduli Scan

A report on twin, cousin and sexy prime pairs in arithmetic progressions, scanned over about 5,000 moduli up to 109. Re-run here with a full sieve to 109 and checked line by line. The owner's twin chain race turns out to be the report's modulus 9.

Checks: math/theorems/pasted_prime_pairs_moduli_scan_audit_2026_11_14.py. Data: records/prime_pairs_1e9_moduli_scan.json.

Verdict

  • RightPrimes, twin and sexy pair counts, and the three cousin classes by 30.
  • WrongThe cousin total is 3,424,680, not 3,424,681. The report's own three classes plus (3, 7) add to 3,424,680.
  • RightThe variance law κ = (log q + 5.92) / log x reproduces on 4,938 moduli.
  • Wrong"The law plus sampling noise explains everything." Measured against the law's own spread, 134 moduli lie beyond 3σ, where about 13 are expected. Prime moduli sit above the law and composite moduli below it.
  • OpenThe owner's chain race (modulus 9) is more spread out than the law at 109 (p = 0.027) and more even at 1012 (p = 0.011). The checkpoints are seven cumulative looks, so neither result decides.

Where the law misses

If counts vary κ times pure chance, as the law says, the sampling spread of κ is κ·√(2/df). That's about 0.03; the report measured against √(2/df), about 0.05, and so found only 6 outliers. Against the right spread:

For primes from 20,000 to 30,000 alone, the scatter matches sampling noise (0.0071 against 0.0070, confirmed by simulation). The split comes from the composite moduli. Adding S(q) = Σ log p/(p − 1) over the primes p dividing q shrinks the twin prime-minus-composite gap from 0.062 to 0.014. Outliers remain, so the law still misses some structure.

That form of the S(q) term is recalled from the lower-order term of the Hooley / Friedlander–Goldston variance for primes in progressions. The 1996 paper was not reachable to confirm it. Related: Goldston and Yıldırım (Canad. J. Math. 1998).

The owner's twin chain race as modulus 9
