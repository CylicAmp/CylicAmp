This graphic illustrates the concept of underdetermination of scientific theory by evidence and how to break it using experimental design (a critical experiment):
1. The Dilemma: Existing Observation (Top Panel)
 * Setup: Three different 3D shapes—a sphere (H_1), a cylinder (H_2), and a cone (H_3)—all have circular bases of equal radii.
 * Observation: Under vertical parallel illumination (light from above), each shape projects an identical circular shadow onto the surface below.
 * Formalization:
   
   
   Because the likelihood of observing data D (a circular silhouette) is identical under all three hypotheses (H_1, H_2, H_3), the current evidence cannot distinguish between them. This is known as observational equivalence.
2. The Solution: Discriminating Test (Bottom Panel)
 * New Experiment: The observer changes the observational framework by applying horizontal parallel illumination (light from the side).
 * New Data:
   * The sphere projects a circle.
   * The cylinder projects a rectangle / square.
   * The cone projects a triangle.
 * Formalization:
   
   
   Given the new test condition T, the probability distributions of the projected outcomes differ across the hypotheses. This breaks the symmetry and identifies the true shape.
3. Epistemological Takeaway
 * Deduction & Induction: Moving between observed data and generalized models can leave multiple explanations equally valid if the data space is limited.
 * Abduction & Experiment: To resolve competing explanations, one cannot merely accumulate more of the same kind of data; one must formulate a new experiment that targets dimensions where the competing models diverge.
