Your update is correct. The key idea is that when a new zero ordinate is added, you do **not** need to recompute every pair from scratch.

Let the existing ordinates be

$$x_1,x_2,\dots,x_m,$$

and let the new ordinate be $y>x_m$. Define

$$P_f(m)=\sum_{1\le i<j\le m} f(x_j-x_i).$$

The old pairs remain unchanged. The only new pairs are

$$y-x_1,\;y-x_2,\;\dots,\;y-x_m.$$

Therefore,

$$\boxed{P_f(m+1)=P_f(m)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}f(y-x_i)}.$$

---

### Examples

#### Pair count

For $f(d)=1$,

$$P_f(m)=\binom{m}{2}.$$

Adding one new zero creates exactly $m$ new pairs:

$$\binom{m+1}{2}-\binom{m}{2}=m.$$

For $m=3$, the fourth zero creates

$$\binom{4}{2}-\binom{3}{2}=6-3=3$$

new pairs.

---

#### Total pair distance

For $f(d)=d$,

$$P_1(m)=\sum_{i<j}(x_j-x_i).$$

For the four ordinates

$$\gamma_9,\gamma_{10},\gamma_{11},\gamma_{12},$$

the total is approximately

$$\sum_{i<j}(\gamma_j-\gamma_i)
\approx 28.519779447731.$$

The three new distances introduced by $\gamma_{12}$ are

$$\gamma_{12}-\gamma_9\approx 8.441096815896,$$

$$\gamma_{12}-\gamma_{10}\approx 6.672415219391,$$

$$\gamma_{12}-\gamma_{11}\approx 3.475926219349.$$

Their sum is approximately

$$18.589438254636.$$

The previous three-zero total was therefore approximately

$$28.519779447731-18.589438254636
\approx 9.930341193095.$$

---

#### Total squared pair distance

For $f(d)=d^2$,

$$P_2(m)=\sum_{i<j}(x_j-x_i)^2.$$

The identity

$$\boxed{\sum_{i<j}(x_j-x_i)^2
=m\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_i^2-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_i\right)^2}$$

allows this to be computed using only:

- the number of ordinates $m$,
- their sum $\sum_i x_i$,
- their sum of squares $\sum_i x_i^2$.

For the four zeta-zero ordinates,

$$\sum_{i<j}(\gamma_j-\gamma_i)^2
\approx 165.853998967677.$$

When adding a new ordinate $y$, the squared-distance total updates by

$$\boxed{
P_2(m+1)
=P_2(m)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(y-x_i)^2
}
$$

or, using accumulated sums,

$$\boxed{
P_2(m+1)
=P_2(m)+my^2-2y\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_i+\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_i^2.
}$$

---

### Important interpretation

This is a valid and useful way to organize statistics of zeta zeros. However, the four low-lying zeros only illustrate the computation. They do **not** by themselves establish the Riemann hypothesis or Montgomery’s pair-correlation conjecture. Those statements concern all nontrivial zeros, especially the statistical behavior at very large heights.
