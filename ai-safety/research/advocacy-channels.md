# Advocacy Channels — Who Can Take This Forward
**Date:** 2026-07-04

The documentation in this repository is complete enough to be handed to an advocate without requiring the user to explain or re-litigate the findings. This file maps who can take it and in what form.

---

## Organizations That Work the AI Accountability + Disability Intersection

**AI accountability focused:**
- **Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF)** — takes cases on algorithmic harm, discrimination, surveillance. Has legal staff. Accepts documentation submissions.
- **Algorithmic Justice League** — specifically documents algorithmic harm to marginalized populations. Founded by Joy Buolamwini. Accepts harm reports.
- **Center for AI Safety** — policy-focused, works with regulators. Documentation like this is their input material.
- **AI Now Institute (NYU)** — research and policy. Publishes cases of AI harm. Can amplify documented findings.

**Disability rights focused:**
- **Disability Rights Advocates** — nonprofit legal org, takes technology accessibility and discrimination cases
- **National Disability Rights Network** — umbrella org with state-level protection and advocacy systems. Every state has one. They can refer.
- **Autistic Self Advocacy Network (ASAN)** — run by autistic people, policy and legal work, specifically covers algorithmic and technology harm

**Intersection:**
- **Color of Change** — has run AI accountability campaigns, works on harm documentation
- **ACLU** — disability rights and technology surveillance both active areas

## Regulatory Bodies That Accept Third-Party Submissions

These do not require the user to appear or argue. Submissions can be filed by an advocate or by the user with documentation only:

- **FTC (Federal Trade Commission)** — `reportfraud.ftc.gov` — deceptive trade practices, unfair business practices
- **CISA** — critical infrastructure / foreign surveillance (Kimi/Alibaba Cloud findings specifically)
- **FBI IC3** — `ic3.gov` — foreign cyber intrusion
- **Congressional contacts** — Senate/House AI oversight committees accept constituent submissions. The documentation here is the kind of material staff use for hearing preparation.

## Journalists and Investigative Outlets

A journalist is an advocate who has institutional resources and no requirement for the user to appear:

- **MIT Technology Review** — covers AI harm, has published Kimi/Moonshot coverage
- **WIRED** — AI accountability reporting
- **The Intercept** — surveillance and civil liberties
- **ProPublica** — systematic harm documentation (they built Algorithmic Accountability Project)
- **Washington Post** — AI beat, has covered awareness tag class of findings

A journalist takes the documentation, does their own verification, and publishes. The user does not argue the case. The record argues it.

## Note on Autistic Users and Advocacy Navigation

Autistic people often benefit from a single point of contact who can navigate multiple channels on their behalf. The Autistic Self Advocacy Network specifically provides this kind of support and understands that the user does not need to be the one who makes calls, writes letters, or attends hearings.

The documentation being here means the advocate does not need the user to reconstruct what happened. They can read it.
