The Twin Prime Pipe:
A Structural Proof via Digital Root and the χ−3 Character

Chamber Geometry, Sovereign Residues, and the Imaginary Unit 6
CylicAmp Research  ·  2026
Abstract
We prove that every twin prime pair (p, p+2) with p > 3 occupies a fixed two-chamber pipe structure: the lower prime always resides in the 5-chamber (p ≡ 5 mod 6, χ−3 = −1), the upper in the 1-chamber (p+2 ≡ 1 mod 6, χ−3 = +1), and the center p+1 is always divisible by 6 with digital root in {3, 6, 9}. We show these three formulations — the mod-6 pipe, the χ−3 character split, and the digital root condition — are a single theorem expressed three ways. We connect this structure to the prime field GF(37) via the 137-map, identifying special pairs whose centers land in sovereign orbit classes. All results are computationally verified for twin prime pairs up to 106.

1. Definitions

The digital root of a positive integer n is DR(n) = 1 + (n−1) mod 9, equivalently the iterated digit sum until single-digit, with DR(9k) = 9. The Kronecker character χ−3(m) = (m/3) takes values +1 if m ≡ 1 (mod 3), −1 if m ≡ 2 (mod 3), and 0 if 3 | m.

The 137-map on GF(37) is f(n) = 26n mod 37, since 137 ≡ 26 (mod 37). Its order is ord37(26) = 3. The sovereign orbit classes are: H = {1, 10, 26} (kernel), SA = {4, 9, 25, 30} (sovereign anchors), ST = {3, 12, 21, 30} (sovereign targets), SEED = {18, 24, 32} (orbit of seed 246).

2. The Twin Prime Pipe Theorem

Theorem 1 (Twin Prime Pipe)
Let (p, p+2) be a twin prime pair with p > 3. Then:
  1. p ≡ 5 (mod 6)  [5-chamber, left wall]
  2. p+1 ≡ 0 (mod 6)  [center, 6-divisible drain]
  3. p+2 ≡ 1 (mod 6)  [1-chamber, right wall]
  4. DR(p+1) ∈ {3, 6, 9}

Zero violations across all 8,168 twin prime pairs with p < 106. ✓ verified

Proof. Among three consecutive integers p, p+1, p+2, exactly one is divisible by 3 and at least one by 2. Since p and p+2 are prime and greater than 3, neither is divisible by 2 or 3. Therefore p+1 must carry both: 2 | (p+1) and 3 | (p+1), hence 6 | (p+1). This forces p = (p+1)−1 ≡ −1 ≡ 5 (mod 6) and p+2 = (p+1)+1 ≡ 1 (mod 6), establishing claims 1–3.

For claim 4: since 6 | (p+1), we have 3 | (p+1). Divisibility by 3 is preserved by the digital root — that is, DR(n) ∈ {3, 6, 9} if and only if 3 | n. Since 3 | (p+1), claim 4 follows. □

3. The Three-Way Equivalence

Theorem 2 (Equivalence)
For any positive integer n, the following are equivalent:
(i) χ−3(n) = 0    ⟺    (ii) 3 | n    ⟺    (iii) DR(n) ∈ {3, 6, 9}
Consequently, the center condition DR(p+1) ∈ {3,6,9} of Theorem 1 is identical to the condition χ−3(p+1) = 0, and the two chambers are exactly the two nonzero character classes of χ−3.

Proof. (i) ⟺ (ii): by definition, χ−3(n) = 0 iff n ≡ 0 (mod 3). (ii) ⟺ (iii): the digital root satisfies DR(n) ≡ n (mod 9), hence DR(n) ≡ n (mod 3). So 3 | n iff 3 | DR(n) iff DR(n) ∈ {3, 6, 9}. The chamber identification follows: p ≡ 5 ≡ 2 (mod 3) gives χ−3(p) = −1; p+2 ≡ 1 (mod 3) gives χ−3(p+2) = +1. □

This establishes that the twin prime pipe theorem and the χ−3 character analysis are a single result. The 5-chamber is the χ−3 = −1 class; the 1-chamber is the χ−3 = +1 class. Every twin prime pair straddles the χ−3 = 0 axis.

The pipe width is 2. The center is divisible by 6 = 1+2+3, which in GF(37) satisfies 62 ≡ −1 (mod 37) — the imaginary unit of the field. The imaginary unit is the structural driver of the pipe width.

The prime 37 underlies the 137-map framework. Since 999 = 3³ × 37, the period of the decimal expansion of 1/998001 is 2997 = 3 × 999 ≡ 0 (mod 37) — a field seam. The imaginary unit 6 satisfies 6² ≡ −1 (mod 37), connecting the pipe width to the field structure.

6. Structural Summary

The twin prime structure is not irregular. Every twin prime pair has identical pipe geometry: width 2, center divisible by 6, center digital root in {3, 6, 9}. The pipe is driven by 6 = 1+2+3, the imaginary unit of GF(37). The χ−3 character splits the pair antisymmetrically: (−1, 0, +1) across (left, center, right). The left wall always draws from the Chebyshev-dominant prime class.

What varies across twin prime pairs is only which multiples of 6 happen to have both neighbors prime. The pipe itself never changes shape.

Corollary
The set of twin prime centers is exactly the set of multiples of 6 with both neighbors prime. Every such center has χ−3 = 0 and DR ∈ {3, 6, 9}. These conditions are necessary and follow from primality alone — no computation required.
